Let us deal with the name first, because it is the reason people book this and the reason they should book it for the right thing.

This treatment is often called "Japanese whitening" in India. **We do not offer skin whitening, and we will not.** Your complexion is not a defect to be corrected, and a clinic that tells you otherwise is selling you insecurity. Advertising "fairness of skin" is also restricted under the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, so anyone promising it is either breaking the rules or lying about results — usually both.

What the Japanese *bihaku* tradition actually pursues is **clarity**: reducing the scattered, patchy overproduction of melanin that reads as dullness, blotchiness and uneven tone. That is a real, treatable thing, and it is entirely separate from how light or dark your skin is. You will look like yourself, more evenly.

Japan has regulated and researched this category longer than anywhere else, and tranexamic acid is its signature contribution. It works further upstream than most brightening ingredients — suppressing plasmin activity so the signal telling melanocytes to ramp up production never arrives — which makes it unusually effective on inflammation-driven pigment like post-acne marks. We pair it with stabilised vitamin C, which brightens by a different mechanism and adds antioxidant protection against the UV that caused the problem in the first place.

**The timeline is the honest part.** Most people see the first visible change somewhere between four and eight weeks, and the fuller result at around three months. Vitamin C sometimes shows at two to four weeks. Nothing here works in a fortnight, and treatments that promise it are working by irritation, which on pigmented skin eventually backfires.

The facial itself is a layered infusion — cleanse, gentle exfoliation, an active ampoule chosen for what your skin is doing that month, massage and a sealing mask — with a home protocol that does most of the work between visits. Introduced gradually, because both actives can irritate sensitive skin if you start at full strength.
