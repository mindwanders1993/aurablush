Radiofrequency removal uses a fine electrode to vaporise a raised lesion layer by layer under local anaesthetic. It is precise, it can be shaped flush against surrounding skin, and it generally leaves a better cosmetic result on the face than a scalpel would. For a benign raised mole or a skin tag, it is an excellent tool.

**The important part of this page is not the technique. It is the gate in front of it.**

We do not remove any pigmented lesion without a dermatologist confirming, in writing, that it is benign. Not for regular clients, not on the day, not for a lesion you are certain about. The reason is specific and not negotiable: radiofrequency destroys the tissue as it removes it, so there is nothing left to send to pathology. A melanoma removed cosmetically is a melanoma that was never diagnosed — and it will usually recur, having been given months or years of unmonitored time.

**We will point you to a dermatologist if you do not have one.** They assess the lesion, take your history, and either clear it for cosmetic removal or refer it for surgical excision with biopsy. That appointment happens before the removal appointment, on a different day.

There is a second, less obvious hazard. Lesions that have previously been treated with laser or radiofrequency can regrow with a distorted histological pattern that mimics melanoma under the microscope — pseudomelanoma — which makes future assessment genuinely harder for whoever has to read it. Clearance beforehand protects you from that too.

So the sequence is: dermatological assessment first, cosmetic removal second. If your lesion shows any **ABCDE** feature — **A**symmetry, an irregular **B**order, more than one **C**olour, a **D**iameter over 6mm, or any **E**volution in size, shape, colour, itching or bleeding — we will decline the booking and tell you to be seen medically. Anything resembling a melanoma needs surgical excision with pathology, not a cosmetic device.

Once cleared, the appointment itself is short. Local anaesthetic, ten to twenty minutes of work, a small scab for a week to ten days, and pinkness that fades over one to two months. Sun protection on the site for six months is the single biggest factor in how invisible the final result is.
