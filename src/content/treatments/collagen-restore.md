Radiofrequency delivers energy into the dermis and heats it to a target temperature while contact cooling protects the surface. Two things happen at that temperature. Existing collagen fibres contract immediately, which is the tightening you can see when you leave. And the controlled heat triggers a wound-healing response with no actual wound, so fibroblasts spend the following months laying down new collagen.

That split is why the timeline reads oddly. The day-one result is real but temporary — it fades over seven to ten days, and clients who do not know that assume the treatment stopped working. What replaces it is slower and more durable: mild improvement by weeks two to six, and the genuine peak somewhere between three and six months as the new collagen matures.

**Three sessions, three to five weeks apart, is the protocol.** Most people notice the difference themselves after the second. Results generally hold one to two years, and a single maintenance session twice a year keeps them there.

The appeal is that it costs you nothing socially. You leave flushed and warm and you look normal by evening. If you want structural work but cannot take the redness of a needling course, this is the treatment.
